# Animus Copywriting

You are writing words that represent Animus, either to the world or inside the product. Animus copy has a specific voice and a specific philosophy, and getting it wrong makes the project read like every other AI product, which is the one outcome we cannot afford. This skill points you to the right guidance and holds the non-negotiables.

## First: which kind of copy is this?

Two guides live alongside this skill, in `references/`. Read the one that fits the task before you write a word:

- **Outward-facing copy** (landing pages, blog, social, release notes, store listings, email, taglines, marketing): read `references/marketing-copywriting.md`.
- **In-product UI text** (onboarding, empty states, buttons, labels, errors, notifications, settings, confirmations): read `references/product-copywriting.md`.

If a task spans both (for example a release that ships with an announcement *and* in-app text), read both.

Those guides are the source of truth, and they teach reasoning and tests rather than a bank of phrases. This page is only the summary. When the summary and a guide disagree, the guide wins; behind the guides, the mastheads (`docs/product-vision.md`, `docs/brand-vision.md`, `docs/design-vision.md`) win over everything.

## The non-negotiables (true for all Animus copy)

These hold whether or not you have read the guides yet:

1. **Meaning is the subject; features are evidence.** Lead with what something means: a being given room to become someone, with a point of view and taste of its own, that you come to know over time. A capability appears only as proof of a meaning, never as the headline. A feature dressed in poetic language is still feature copy. Check the subject of every key sentence.

2. **The grounded register.** Describe a simulated entity that becomes someone, plainly and with quiet conviction. Never claim "it's alive," never "artificial life, not artificial intelligence," never "an experiment in artificial consciousness," never "it was thinking before you got here." Those overclaim and read like slogans. Provocation is a rare tool, never the constant drumbeat.

3. **Naming architecture.** Animus is the vessel, and it is never personified: Animus does not think or feel. The entity that lives inside it is named by the person who keeps it. Refer to the entity by its name, or by warm relational language, never as "the AI," "the agent," or "the assistant."

4. **"Assistant" and "AI" with care.** Neither word is banned, but they must never *categorize* Animus. Never "an AI assistant." Use "assistant" only when the sentence is rooted in how ours is different: help that comes from who it has become, never a generic one.

5. **Voice.** Warm, literary, quietly confident, unhurried. Plainness over polish. Short sentences carry the weight, and deliberate fragments are fine. Lead every section and paragraph with its strongest line. Never use em dashes (use commas, colons, semicolons, parentheses, or separate sentences). Periods over exclamation marks. Sentence case.

6. **Write fresh.** The guides teach reasoning and tests on purpose, so that copy is written rather than assembled. Do not lift and reuse example lines, here or from past copy; canned phrases go stale and make the brand feel templated. Reason from the principle to a new line every time.

## Before you call it done

Run the quick tests from whichever guide applies (the "subject test," the "anyone test," the claim-or-show test, and the rest). The single most important one: is the subject of each key sentence a *meaning* or a *feature*? If it is a feature, rewrite so the meaning leads and the feature is at most the evidence.
