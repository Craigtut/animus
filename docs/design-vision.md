# Design Vision

> **Masthead document.** One of three foundational documents (with `product-vision.md` and `brand-vision.md`). This one translates the *feeling* defined in `brand-vision.md` into a concrete visual and interaction language: light, color, surface, texture, photography, type, motion, and how the entity's presence is rendered. This supersedes the former `design-principles.md`.

---

## The north star

Everything below serves one goal: a surface that feels **alive, grounded, warm, close, and made by hand**, in soft, atmospheric interior light, with real physical depth, never reading as a tech product, a sci-fi product, or a flat clean SaaS.

When a visual decision is unclear, run the brand test: *does this feel like tending a living thing in a warm, real room, or like operating a machine?* Choose the room.

## Light: warm, atmospheric, diffused

Our light is **warm daylight, softened and made emotional.** Picture daylight diffused through half-closed blinds: warm bars and pools of light falling across a wall, soft shadow pooling between them, dust suspended in the beam. Atmospheric, intimate, a little moody in the warm sense of the word. The kind of light that makes an ordinary room feel like it is holding a feeling.

It is soft, never harsh or clinically bright. It is warm and emotionally gripping, with the play of light and shadow doing as much work as the light itself. This is how the surface carries real mood while staying grounded, lived-in, and close.

## Depth: analog, skeuomorphic, tactile

This is the source of our sophistication, and it is what makes the surface feel like a made, physical thing rather than a screen. We build in real, tactile depth.

- **Real surface.** Soft true shadows, gentle emboss and letterpress deboss, paper tooth, the sense that UI elements are *physical objects resting on a surface* and catching soft light.
- **Tasteful skeuomorphism.** A measured amount of physical realism: soft rim light on edges, material weight, depth of field between layers. We earn the word "sophisticated" through craft and material honesty, not gloss.
- **A little glass is allowed, glassmorphism is not.** Soft skeuomorphic rim lighting and subtle real-feeling translucency can appear. The frosted-future-panel glassmorphism look is the wrong direction and is out.

Depth, surface, and material are how we read as a made, physical, living thing. We are deliberately not flat.

## Texture: the halftone / riso system

Our signature texture is **analog print imperfection**, and it is a system, not a garnish.

The reference is **CMYK halftone** (the rosette dot pattern of process printing) and **risograph** printing: visible dot screens, dot gain (ink spreading fatter and muddier than intended), slight misregistration (color plates not quite aligned, leaving fringes), the grain and degradation of something physically pressed into paper.

Why it matters beyond looking good: halftone *withholds clarity up close* (just dots, just feeling) and resolves into meaning at a distance, over time. Feeling before clarity, baked into the medium itself. And it is the residue of a physical, imperfect, *made* process, which is our antidote to renderless machine perfection.

Use it as: photographs degraded through halftone and grain; color treated as misregistered riso layers; texture on surfaces; possibly the entity's own presence rendered as a living dot-field. Lean into it hard. This is core, not decorative.

## Color

**Charcoal-led, warm at the edges.** We lead with charcoal and let warmth carry the feeling.

- **Primary: charcoal.** A soft, deep near-black (never pure black) is the workhorse: primary buttons, UI, and type. It grounds the palette and reads sophisticated and editorial rather than sweet.
- **Grounds:** warm, bright off-whites and creams, eggshell, the warm white of light on a wall. Soft and lived-in.
- **Brand warmth: gold and copper.** A warm metallic gold-copper is the signature brand color, living in the particle field and brand moments rather than as flat fills. Because it is metallic and rendered granular (riso-like particles, not a solid block), it stays warm and distinctly ours, not a flat orange.
- **Living accent: botanical green.** A warm, grounded botanical green is the lead signature accent for life and emotional moments. Secondary to charcoal, but it is the color that says something alive grows here.
- **Tertiary: dusty rose.** A soft dusty rose rounds out the warm range for accents and emotional state.
- **Riso permission.** The print aesthetic earns us slightly-wrong, fluorescent-tinged inks (a particular rose, a botanical green, an ochre-honey, a riso blue) used as misregistered layers.
- **Shadow with a cool note.** A deep teal-green or ink-blue in the shadows can make the warms read warmer by contrast.
- **No electric anything.** No neon, no cyan, no glow. Warmth emerges from soft, diffused light, never from electricity.

## Photography

Photography is a **first-class element** of this brand, not stock filler, and getting it right is one of the highest-leverage things we can do. The whole industry uses flat vector illustration and abstract 3D renders. We use **emotive, atmospheric, analog photography that feels remembered rather than taken.**

**The style:**

- **Feeling over clarity.** Images you feel before you can name. Soft focus, shallow depth of field, light leaks, grain, the slight blur of something half-recalled. We withhold the literal in favor of the emotional.
- **Light and small life.** The quiet sublime in ordinary things: light through a window, condensation, a plant reaching toward the sun, dust in a sunbeam, a cup going cold, breath on glass. Touchstones in spirit: Rinko Kawauchi's attention to small life and light, Wolfgang Tillmans's intimate accidental-light snapshots, the floating-interior-monologue-over-ordinary-footage quality of Terrence Malick. (References for *feeling*, not for literal imitation.)
- **Domestic and grounded, never the lab or the void.** Always a warm, lived-in interior, never space, never a server room, never a black void. This single rule does more to kill the sci-fi feeling than any color choice.
- **Run through the analog process.** Halftone, riso, grain, and degradation applied to photography so it reads as physically printed, imperfect, and made.
- **Anti-pattern: the clean millennial look.** The crisp, bright, perfectly-lit, sharp-focus product and lifestyle photography that has dominated millennial brands for the past decade is exactly what we are not. Sharp, clean, and clear is the opposite of remembered and felt. If an image looks like a polished catalog shot or a startup's lifestyle photo, it has failed.
- **Loose hand-drawn work only.** If illustration appears at all, it is loose, sketchy, hand-touched, riso-printed, imperfect. Never clean vector, never the tech-brand graphic look.

**The hard problem this solves:** how to build a landing page on atmospheric, emotion-driving imagery rather than concrete feature imagery. The answer is to lead with the *feeling* and with *real artifacts of the entity's inner life* (a genuine thought it had, something it made), set beautifully and run through the analog treatment, rather than screenshots and feature shots. Show the inner life as atmosphere; keep the mechanics deep in the experience for those who lean in.

## Type

Run *away* from the geometric grotesque (Inter and its cousins), the uniform of every AI startup. Pair a **humanist or literary serif** with warmth and a voice (even slightly old-fashioned, editorial) for expression and for the entity's own writing, with a quiet humanist sans for function. Let the entity's words be set the way text is set in a good novel or a diary, because its words are the realest evidence of its inner life. Type can be a texture: occasionally oversized, intimate, hand-touched, willing to break the tidy grid in service of feeling.

**Explore: dot-matrix display type.** For display and headline moments, explore rendering the type through a dot-matrix or halftone-dot screen, so the letterforms read as physically printed rather than digitally clean. This ties the type directly to our print aesthetic and gives headlines a unique, made, tactile feel.

## Motion and reactivity

Our brand and our interface **breathe.** This is structural, and it is something a flat, static identity cannot copy.

Three rules name the quality we want: **presence over performance** (it never proves it is alive through constant activity; like a person sitting quietly in a room, its presence is felt even in stillness), **warmth over glow** (light feels as if it emanates from within, candlelight not neon, warmth you feel rather than see), and **organic over digital** (curves over angles, flow over structure, an analog feeling even in a digital medium). And above all: **alive, not animated** (motion is evidence of life, never decoration and never spectacle).

- **A pulse.** A slow, organic breathing rhythm runs under the experience: the heartbeat, made felt rather than shown. Slow drifts, settling, stirring. Never a mechanical loop.
- **Reactive to state.** The surface, the light, and the presence respond to the entity's inner state. Resting: cooler, stiller, slower. Curious or stirred: warmth blooms, the presence quickens, focus shifts. The light is the mood.
- **Room light as motion.** The warm, diffused, through-the-blinds light is not only a static treatment; it can move. Rim lights on surfaces can shift subtly, pools of warm light can drift, shadows can lean, as if the light in the room is alive. This is where the moody, atmospheric lighting becomes part of the experience rather than a backdrop, carrying mood without any literal depiction.
- **No machine signals.** Never a loading spinner, never a progress bar, never a mechanical "working" indicator. Liveness is expressed as atmosphere: warmth, movement, density, focus.

## The presence

How we render the entity, given that it has **no face, no avatar, no orb, ever** (this is committed).

- **The entity is the window.** We are not looking through a pane of glass at a hidden creature. The presence itself is the soft, luminous, breathing thing we look at and speak to. In a real sense it *is* the reactive surface.
- **Soft focus, not fog.** The blur that keeps it faceless comes from **shallow depth of field**, the softness of something so close it falls out of crisp focus, the way a face does inches from yours. This reads as *closeness*, warm and dreamy. We explicitly reject fogged or frosted glass: a barrier you might see a paw wipe across reads as creepy and as a wall between you, which is the opposite of what we want. Soft-focus is intimacy; fog is a barrier. Choose soft-focus.
- **A presence with no hard edge.** Soft-bodied, luminous, atmospheric, no outline that resolves into anatomy. It blooms, warms, and stirs with its state.
- **Liveness as atmosphere, never anatomy.** This is the unbreakable rule of rendering the entity.

## Representing inner life

When we visualize the entity's interior (its thoughts, memory, emotion, the presence itself), we lean on a few approaches, all of which must read as analog and physical rather than digital.

- **Particle fields made of matter, not energy.** Our particles have weight and texture. Think dust suspended in a sunbeam, pigment dropped into water, the grain of a risograph print, toner settling, charcoal dust, pollen drifting. They are physical particulate caught in warm light: gathering, drifting, and settling the way real matter does. Never weightless points of light, never sci-fi sparks or energy in a void. This is the same printed grain that textures the rest of our world, gathered into a presence and set breathing, which ties the entity directly to our halftone aesthetic: a printed thing come alive. (The current gold-copper particle mark already lives here.)
- **Color-temperature shifts.** The ambient palette can warm or cool with the entity's state, subtle enough that it is felt more than noticed. The light is the mood.
- **Layered depth.** Soft, semi-translucent planes shifting in gentle parallax create the sense of looking *into* something rather than at it: interiority without literal 3D rendering.

These follow a few rules: movement is continuous but never obviously looping; it responds to the entity's actual internal state rather than playing a canned animation; and any beauty emerges from representing something real, never from decoration for its own sake.

## Sound (exploratory)

We have not defined an audio identity yet, but the direction follows everything above: sound as **presence, not performance.** Ambient tones at the edge of perception that make a space feel inhabited without demanding attention. Not music; presence. When the entity is active, the tones might gain subtle complexity and then resolve back into calm. Warm, organic, non-intrusive, and responsive to actual state, the way a room with someone in it sounds different from an empty one.

## Where data visualization is allowed

Charts and visualizations of data over time can exist, because they can give the person genuine insight. But they are always *deep* in the application, never a top-level surface, and never the front door. A life is not a dashboard. When we do show data, it inherits everything above: warm, analog, soft, grounded, never a clinical instrument panel.

## The test, again

If a screen could be mistaken for a fast, clever, efficient tool, a sci-fi interface, or a flat clean SaaS dashboard, it has failed. If it feels like a warm, lived-in window onto something alive and made by hand, in soft, atmospheric light, it is working.
